(03)the origin[ murphy's law for ai ]

murphy's law was a design brief, not a mood.

1949, a rocket-sled test for the us air force. a set of sensors turned out to be installed the wrong way round. the takeaway, as the story is usually told: if it can be done wrong, someone will do it wrong.

the engineering answer was not 'be more careful'. it was: make the wrong way impossible.

wrong way: blocked

right way: fits

sim card: the cut corner fits one way only.

no wrong side

usb-c: designed so there is no wrong way in.

only fits its hole

poka-yoke: toyota's word for mistake-proofing.

  3. 03a form, not a blank page.

and murphy's law was never meant as a mood. the story as it's usually told: 1949, a rocket-sled test for the us air force, and a set of sensors installed the wrong way round. the takeaway: if it can be done wrong, someone will do it wrong. the engineering answer wasn't "be more careful". it was to make the wrong way impossible. a sim card has a cut corner so it only fits one way. usb-c removed the wrong way entirely. toyota has a word for it, poka-yoke. for us with ai, that means constrain before you generate: a template, a schema, the real system names, the non-goals. a form, not a blank page.

(05)buffers[ murphy's law for ai ]

slack looks like waste until the bad week.

retail keeps safety stock because demand and delivery both wobble. on a good week it just sits there. on a bad week it is the business.

in ai work

  1. 01a second model attacks the draft cold.
  2. 02review capped at two rounds, then a human decides.
  3. 03time booked for checking, not only for making.

this one should feel familiar, because we're retail. safety stock exists because demand wobbles and deliveries wobble. on a good week the buffer looks like waste, money sitting on a shelf. then you get a week like this one: a demand spike plus a late truck. stock dips into the buffer and not to zero, and the customer never notices. ai work needs the same slack. a second model that attacks the draft cold, not the one that wrote it. review capped at two rounds and then a human decides, so we don't loop forever. and real time booked for checking. ai saved us the making time, so spend some of it here.

(06)recovery[ murphy's law for ai ]

design the fall, not just the climb.

1854, new york. elisha otis rode a lifting platform up in front of a crowd, then had the rope cut. the platform held.

otis did not invent the elevator. he made its failure survivable.

[ how it works ]

  1. 01the rope's pull keeps a spring flexed and the catches tucked in.
  2. 02the rope snaps. the spring snaps back.
  3. 03its ends push two catches into the teeth on the rails.
  4. 04the car stops after a short drop.

in ai work

  1. 01everything ai touches lives in version control. undo is one step.
  2. 02prototypes stay behind a gate until a human checks them.
  3. 03when it breaks: a clear status and a next step, never a blank screen.

1854, new york. elisha otis rode a lifting platform up in front of a crowd, then had the rope cut. the platform held. elevators already existed. what otis added was a survivable failure: when the rope goes slack, a spring throws catches into teeth on the rails. people started trusting elevators because the fall was designed. for ai work, that means everything it touches lives in version control, so undo is one step. prototypes stay behind a gate until someone checks them. and when something breaks, the user gets a status and a next step, not a blank screen.
