competition background & problem statement

For the Schneider Go Green 2022 competition, our student team, Team Kancil from Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB), examined unreliable electricity in remote Indonesian villages such as Karya Jadi, South Kalimantan, where solar batteries deteriorate within 2 to 3 years. Our concept proposal, HotSand, explored using locally sourced dry sand as a thermal energy storage medium paired with a Stirling engine for off-grid rural villages.

proposal overview [ concept & thermal storage ]
sand-based thermal storage proposal
The competition submission proposed using electric heating to store solar generation in insulated local dry sand, providing a longer-lifespan, recyclable alternative to chemical batteries.
stirling engine rural microgrid concept
The model explored pairing a 330 kg dry sand storage core with a Stirling engine to dispatch 5 kVA of nighttime electricity for 10 households, evaluated against the remote off-grid village of Karya Jadi in South Kalimantan, designed for potential integration with Schneider EcoStruxure.
awards & context [ competition outcomes ]
competition recognition
Submitted to Schneider Go Green 2022, the HotSand proposal was recognized as Country Favorite Winner for Indonesia, highlighting its focus on rural electrification and local material utilization.
academic concept note
This work was an academic competition proposal developed during Industrial Engineering studies at Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB). It represents student techno-commercial modeling and systems design, not a tested commercial installation or physical field deployment.
