the core idea

remote archipelagic islands in indonesia often struggle with reliable power. importing chemical lithium batteries involves high capital costs, fire risks, and rapid capacity loss in tropical heat. local silica sand is abundant, safe, and stores thermal energy exceptionally well.

real-world failure handling [ 03 edge cases ]
3 days of rain with minimal sun
during extended rainy periods with minimal sunshine, the insulated sand core keeps holding heat to power the village and dry crops without diesel.
handling sudden demand drops
when local electricity demand drops sharply, excess power is diverted into the sand core as extra thermal reserve, avoiding mechanical strain on the Stirling generator.
maintenance without battery degradation
chemical lithium cells slowly degrade or lose charge when disconnected. sand retains stored heat for weeks, giving technicians plenty of time for mechanical repairs.
