Schneider HotSand: Storing Clean Solar Energy in Simple Sand

By Ramadhana Bhanuharya Krishnamurti • Award Winner • Open Presentation in Drive ↗

💡 The Problem in Plain English

Solar panels work great during the day, but remote island villages and rural farming communities also need energy at night—for heating, drying agricultural harvests, and hot water.

Chemical lithium-ion batteries are expensive to import, wear out quickly in hot tropical humidity, and are dangerous to dispose of. Our solution, HotSand, uses cheap, locally available silica sand beds as a "sand battery." During peak sunlight, solar power heats the sand up to 500°C. That stored heat can be safely stored for weeks and piped out on demand whenever farmers or households need it.

How the Sand Energy Battery Works

A closed loop with zero rare minerals, zero toxic chemicals, and 100% local materials.

STAGE 01
Solar Energy Influx
Daytime solar panels or concentrated solar mirrors collect bright sunlight.
STAGE 02
Resistive Heating
Electricity powers rugged ceramic coils that superheat local silica sand up to 500°C.
STAGE 03
Thermal Insulation
Insulated double-walled silos hold the heat for days with minimal thermal loss.
STAGE 04
On-Demand Dispatch
Air pipes circulate through the sand to provide drying heat and electricity at night.
