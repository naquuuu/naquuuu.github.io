"Western software assumes 24/7 cloud connectivity, fiber grids, and air-conditioned server racks. An archipelago of 17,000 islands demands frugal, rugged cyber-physical infrastructure that stays alive offline."

The Archipelago Mandate: Frugal Cyber-Physical Infrastructure

Imported enterprise hardware frequently breaks down or remains cost-prohibitive in Indonesia’s 3T (frontier, outermost, underdeveloped) regions, inter-island sea transit routes, and rural supply chains. When a fishing vessel crosses the Banda Sea for 18 hours without cellular signal, cold-chain telemetry cannot ping AWS. It must compute anomaly thresholds on-device.

My mission is engineering Frugal Cyber-Physical Infrastructure built around local supply chains and TKDN domestic standards: ruggedized TinyML edge sensor nodes, decentralized cold-chain telemetry, and battery-free energy storage. To bridge this gap from pure digital logic into physical hardware, my academic trajectory targets Innovation Design Engineering (Imperial College London MSc & Royal College of Art MA)—mastering embedded firmware, DFM fabrication, and tactile non-screen physical interfaces.

The Physical Atelier: Art & Canvas

acrylic_on_canvas.art

Friction Between Organic Texture & Deterministic Systems

Original Painting by Krishna • Acrylic & Oil on Canvas

I paint with heavy body acrylics and oils on textured linen. In software, every conditional branch must resolve deterministically; in painting, the tactile drag of a palette knife across raw canvas introduces friction, chemical viscosity, and human spontaneity.

This physical practice is what keeps me grounded as a cyber-physical builder. When you work with physical pigments, you respect the material limits of the medium. The same discipline applies when designing physical enclosures, tactile switches, or edge sensor housings: atoms cannot be refactored with a git commit; they must be crafted with intention.

Grassroots Empathy & Leadership

human_foundation.log

From Answering 10,000 Students to Building Autonomous Squads

Right after graduating high school, my first real professional exposure wasn't an executive internship—it was a customer service frontline role at EdukaSystem, answering the panic, confusion, and dreams of over 10,000+ high school students across Indonesia preparing for university entrance exams.

Answering thousands of real human beings teaches you something textbooks cannot: users don't care about your technology stack; they care whether your product solves their anxiety in seconds. Every complaint was a signal, and every bug was an opportunity to redesign the UX flow. That radical user empathy became the foundation of everything I build.

Later, when I was elected Division Head of Professional Development at MTI ITB, I inherited a 20-person team locked in rigid functional silos and online fatigue. Instead of forcing more Zoom webinars, I dissolved the silos and restructured the team into product-focused squads where members rotated across dynamic product ownership. We prioritized high-touch offline partnerships—partnering with Huawei, Tokopedia, Paragon, and Zekindo to organize walk-in interviews and company immersions for 200+ students. Our division went on to rank #1 out of 15 divisions in MTI ITB with the highest work engagement score under the UWES-17 model.
